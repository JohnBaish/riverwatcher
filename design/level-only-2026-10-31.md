# Riverwatcher: gauges with no history and no typical range

Saturday October 31st 2026, Claude Code. Four weeks of live readings (October 3rd to 31st) for the 422 gauges with neither archive history nor an EA typical range, and for 149 ordinary gauges picked at random to compare. Two of the 422 and one of the 149 didn't answer.

## What the readings show

| | No history or range (420) | Ordinary gauges (148) |
|---|---|---|
| Typical range over the four weeks | 28 cm | 38 cm |
| Moved 10 cm or more | 339 (81%) | 130 (88%) |
| Moved less than 2 cm | 11 | 3 |
| The same reading nearly every time (more than 90%) | 19 | 4 |
| Typical change within a day | 2 cm | 3 cm |

So most behave like rivers. Most are the EA's own operational sites (sluices, outfalls, pumping stations, flood storage areas, and river sites named only after a place), not shown on GOV.UK's Check for flooding (24 of the 422 are).

## John's decision (October 31st): as Claude advised

1. Kept, but the nearest five are chosen from gauges with a judgement first; gauges with neither history nor range only make up the numbers.
2. Shown with their level, movement and chart, and a plain line instead of the judgement (wording to settle in stage 3).
3. Left out when they sit flat or seem stuck: no change of more than 2 cm in four weeks, or the same reading more than 90% of the time. To be worked out by the collector from its own readings (stage 2), so a gauge comes back if it starts moving.
